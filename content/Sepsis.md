Life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection[¹](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2016.0287)

Criteria:
- 2 or more [[Systemic Inflammatory Response Syndrome|SIRS]] criteria
- Suspected or confirmed source of infection
- Organ dysfunction
	- Acute change in [[Sequential Organ Failure Assessment Score|SOFA Score]] ≥ 2 points **OR**
	- [[Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services|CMS]] criteria:
		- [[Lactic Acid]] > 2
		- SBP < 90 mm Hg or [[Mean Arterial Pressure|MAP]] < 65 mm Hg
		- Acute respiratory failure ([[Bilevel Positive Airway Pressure|BiPAP]], [[Continuous Positive Airway Pressure|CPAP]], [[Intubation]])
		- [[Platelet]] < 100,000 and [[International Normalized Ratio|INR]] > 1.5
		- [[Activated Partial Thromboplastin Time|aPTT]] > 60
		- [[Creatinine]] > 2
		- [[Bilirubin]] > 2
		- [[Urine Output]] < 0.5 mL/kg/hr

Treatment:
- IV broad-spectrum antibiotic w/in 1 hr
	- Do not delay antibiotic for blood culture
- [[Fluid Resuscitation]]
- At 6 hrs, assess 2 of the following:
	- [[Central Venous Pressure|CVP]]
	- [[Central Venous Oxygen Saturation]]
	- Bedside cardiac ultrasound
	- [[Passive Leg Raise]]
- #antipyretic can be used for pain control, symptom control (many don't like the feeling of a fever), or other indications for temperature control e.g. neuro-critical-care patients or post-[[Cardiac Arrest]] [²](https://www.doi.org/10.1097/CCM.0000000000007075)

>[!warning]
>#antipyretic can mask worsening or new fever, delaying diagnosis
